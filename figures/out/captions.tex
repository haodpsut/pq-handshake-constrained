\newcommand{\capSizeRatio}{EDHOC's size advantage over DTLS~1.3 across the nine NIST parameter sets. Dashed: the classical ratio in signature mode ($4.14\times$). Dotted: Static DH ($8.85\times$), a mode with no post-quantum variant, since methods~1--3 of RFC~9528 rest on static Diffie--Hellman.}
\newcommand{\capExchanges}{Exchanges against handshake message size. EDHOC over CoAP uses lock-step block-wise transfer, so its count grows linearly; DTLS~1.3 is flight-based and holds at 2 exchanges whatever the size [RFC~9147]. Open circles are measured on an independent CoAP implementation, agreeing with the model in 7 of 7 cases. The DTLS figure is cited, not measured here.}
\newcommand{\capBlocksize}{Sweep of every block size RFC~7959 permits, for an ML-KEM-768 with ML-DSA-65 handshake. Stars mark each curve's optimum. The shaded region and the vertical rule at 64~B mark RIOT's compile-time ceiling (\texttt{CONFIG\_NANOCOAP\_BLOCK\_SIZE\_MAX}); block sizes to its right require rebuilding the firmware. The two panels place their optima differently, so no single setting is best for both latency and energy.}
\newcommand{\capImplementations}{Three DTLS implementations on a constrained link; the dotted rule is the payload of an IEEE~802.15.4 frame (102~B). Cells completing a handshake: gnutls 17/21; mbedtls 14/14; openssl 9/21. The three are bounded by different quantities, so this characterises the implementations rather than the DTLS protocol.}
\newcommand{\capPerMessage}{Size of each EDHOC message. The dashed rule is the payload of an IEEE~802.15.4 frame (102~B). The middle bars are the floor of the argument: the lightest post-quantum variant conceivable, ML-KEM-512 with classical authentication and credentials by reference. Even there the encapsulation key alone is 800~B, $7.8\times$ the frame payload, so block-wise engages in message\_1 under any design.}
