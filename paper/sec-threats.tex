\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\textbf{No constrained hardware.} Every measurement here runs on a desktop over loopback. That
bounds what may be claimed, and the claims are bounded accordingly: exchange counts are a
property of the transport, and no statement is made about latency, energy, or memory, all of
which have been measured on real hardware by
others~\cite{claeys2021,fraile2021,fraile2025}. Loopback also has no propagation delay, so a
timer-driven failure such as the GnuTLS boundary may sit elsewhere on a real link. Ratios are
taken in direction changes throughout, since a CoAP exchange is a request and a response and
therefore two of them; comparing exchanges against direction changes halves every ratio.

\textbf{One implementation-credential pair excluded.} mbedTLS cannot load the largest
certificate: \texttt{mbedtls\_x509\_crt\_parse\_file} rejects the key, which exceeds the
library's configured maximum integer size. Its cells would read as failures at every MTU
including the widest, looking exactly like a result about the link, when the limit is reached
before the link is touched; the per-pair control of Section~\ref{sec:setup} removed that pair.
The same limit dictates how the DTLS baseline is built: the handshake is enlarged with
certificate \emph{chains}, a leaf and its issuer concatenated into one Certificate message, so
the message grows while each key stays inside the bound.

\textbf{Findings are about implementations, not about DTLS.} The three libraries are bounded by
different quantities at very different points, so nothing here supports a statement about the
protocol. The DTLS behaviour compared against in Section~\ref{sec:results} is measured on
DTLS~1.2, not taken from a specification. RFC~9147 is cited only where DTLS~1.3 itself is
described, and the letter notes that no available implementation offers DTLS~1.3 over
datagrams, so that specified figure could not be reproduced here.

\textbf{Loss is assumed independent}, which is optimistic for a low-power radio: bursty loss
favours larger blocks and would move the optimum. The sweep's conclusion does not rest on the
loss model, holding across the whole range plotted including the lossless case.

\textbf{The frame payload is derived, not estimated.} Section~\ref{sec:setup} takes it from
RFC~4944 as \numFramePayloadFrame~B of \texttt{aMaxPHYPacketSize} less \numMacOverhead~B of
\texttt{aMaxFrameOverhead}. Enabling link-layer security reduces it further to
\numFramePayloadSecured~B, so every threshold reported here is the conservative one.

\textbf{What the tuning space does recover.} The largest permitted block size brings the
penalty to $\numRatioBest\times$ on direction changes and $\numRatioFrames\times$ on
airtime. The claim is not that no setting helps, but that the ones that help are unreachable
on the stacks examined, \numCoapBlock~B being both the shipped default and RIOT's
compile-time ceiling.

\textbf{Scope is EDHOC over CoAP.} The lock-step behaviour follows from CoAP block-wise
transfer, so it applies to the profile of~\cite{rfc9668} and not to EDHOC over another
transport. The per-message decomposition reproduces the published total to within
\numDecompError\%, adequate for counting exchanges but not for quoting absolute sizes, which
are taken from the published comparison instead.
