Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed letter, ``Does the Lightweight Handshake Stay Lightweight?
EDHOC, DTLS and Post-Quantum Credentials on IEEE~802.15.4 Links'', for publication in IEEE
Networking Letters. The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not
been published previously.

\textbf{What the letter reports.} EDHOC is deployed on constrained links on the grounds that
it is smaller than DTLS. We ask whether that argument survives post-quantum credentials, and
find that the question is not one of degree. An ML-KEM-512 encapsulation key alone is
\numKemFiveOneTwo~B against a \numFramePayload~B IEEE~802.15.4 frame payload, so CoAP
block-wise transfer engages in message\_1 at every standardised parameter set, whatever
authentication is chosen. At the block size both constrained stacks ship, the handshake takes
\numExchTotalPQ{} exchanges against the \numDtlsMeasTurns{} direction changes we measure for
DTLS.

A second result arrived from checking the first, and is independent of post-quantum
considerations entirely. Of three widely used DTLS implementations, one cannot complete a
handshake at an 802.15.4 frame payload with any credential size, classical ones included; the
three are bounded by different quantities, and none offers DTLS~1.3 over datagrams four years
after RFC~9147 specified it.

\textbf{Why this venue.} The letter is a measurement study of protocol behaviour and security
protocol deployment, both named in the journal's scope, and it is the size of a letter rather
than of a longer paper: one mechanism, measured on two sides, with the tuning space swept to
show what the mechanism does and does not explain.

\textbf{What we do not claim.} Nothing here measures latency, energy, or memory; those have
been measured on real hardware by others and we cite rather than repeat them. Nothing here
supports a statement about DTLS as a protocol rather than about three implementations of it,
and the letter says so where the result might invite that reading. Where tuning substantially
recovers the transport penalty we report that too, alongside the reason it is unavailable in
practice.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} All code, measurements and result files are at
\url{https://github.com/haodpsut/pq-handshake-constrained}. Every figure, table and numerical
value in the letter is generated from the released result files by a single command, and the
repository contains the checks that enforce this. Reproducing the work requires no quantum
hardware and no post-quantum library: the argument concerns message size, so credentials are
padded to post-quantum dimensions rather than computed.

Thank you for considering this submission.
